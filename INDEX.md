# Junior SDE DSA Pattern Catalogue — Product-Based Companies

The goal is to cover the patterns you need for junior software engineering interviews at product-based companies, from foundational problems to challenging interview questions.

**The organizing principle:** learn each pattern deeply enough to recognize when to apply it, rather than solving hundreds of superficially different questions.

Each entry will have:

- **Priority:** P1 (essential), P2 (important), P3 (lower-frequency or advanced).
- **Difficulty:** Easy, Medium, Hard.
- **Variations:** Distinct forms of the pattern you should recognize.
- **Coverage:** Representative problems that test the pattern, not an exhaustive question bank.

Priority is a practical preparation estimate, not a universal company ranking. Interview expectations vary by company and level.

### Important variations

**Prefix sums**

- Range sum queries.
- Subarray sum equals `k`.
- Count subarrays with a particular sum.
- Longest subarray satisfying a sum condition.
- Prefix XOR problems.

**In-place manipulation**

- Move zeroes.
- Remove duplicates.
- Rotate an array.
- Merge sorted arrays.
- Rearrange elements according to a condition.

**Hashing**

- Two Sum.
- Grouping by frequency or signature.
- Longest consecutive sequence.
- Counting pairs with a target sum.
- Detecting repeated prefix states.

**Targeted coverage:** Learn to recognize the underlying operation, not just memorize solutions to individual array questions.

# 3. How to use this catalogue

Do not treat every row as one question or assume every pattern deserves equal practice.

For each pattern:

1. Understand the underlying idea.
2. Solve one easy problem to establish the mechanics.
3. Solve two or three variations to test recognition.
4. Attempt a medium problem without hints.
5. Revisit the pattern later and solve a problem in a mixed, timed session.

A practical initial target is **roughly 120–180 carefully selected problems**, adjusted to your current skill and target companies. That is a planning estimate, not a guarantee of interview readiness. Curated interview plans similarly emphasize representative coverage rather than solving every available question. [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/2163754/study-plan-75-questions-till-interview-ready/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Recommended preparation order

**Phase 1:** Arrays, hashing, strings, two pointers and sliding window.

**Phase 2:** Binary search, sorting, linked lists, stacks and queues.

**Phase 3:** Trees, BSTs and heaps.

**Phase 4:** Graph traversal, topological sorting, shortest paths and DSU.

**Phase 5:** Recursion, backtracking, greedy algorithms and dynamic programming.

**Phase 6:** Tries, bit manipulation, advanced patterns and company-specific practice.

## Important limitation

This is a **pattern inventory**, not a verified database of every question asked by product-based companies. The priorities are general preparation guidance; specific companies may emphasize different topics.
